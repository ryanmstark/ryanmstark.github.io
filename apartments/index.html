Scam checklist: skip a listing if it hits any of these
  • Price well below the neighborhood. A large 1BR with parking for hundreds less than similar units is the most common bait.
  • No showing. The "owner" is out of town, abroad or on a mission, and offers to mail keys after you pay.
  • Money before a tour or lease. Requests for a deposit, "holding fee" or first month by wire, Zelle, Venmo, gift card or crypto. Legitimate landlords take payment after you've seen the unit and signed.
  • No name, address or phone. Email-only contact, an address that's withheld, or contact details that differ from the same photos posted elsewhere.
  • Copied listing. Reverse-search the photos. If they appear on Zillow or a broker site under a different contact or price, it's a hijacked listing.
  • Pressure and sensitive info up front. "Lots of interest, send money today," or asking for your SSN or bank details before you've met anyone.
  • Check ownership. For a by-owner condo, ask for the owner's name and compare it with the SF Assessor-Recorder records before paying anything.

Rent control: what to look for
  • In SF, units built on or before June 13, 1979 may have rent control (capped annual increases). The best bets are flats in older 2+ unit buildings, like 339 Lyon below.
  • Most condos and single-family homes rented on or after Jan 1, 1996 have eviction protection but not rent control, so the rent can go up freely at renewal.
  • Newer construction (e.g. the 2019 Dogpatch building) isn't rent controlled.
  • To confirm a unit's status, ask for the year built or call the Rent Board at 415-252-4600. SF rental laws ↗
Questions to ask every landlord
  • Is the parking spot assigned to this unit, and does it fit a compact sedan (a Mazda 3 sedan is about 15 ft long)? Tandem or shared?
  • Is parking included, or what is the monthly cost?
  • Does the bedroom have an exterior window?
  • Year built, and is the unit covered by rent control?
  • Any required monthly fees beyond rent (utilities, trash, amenity fees)?
